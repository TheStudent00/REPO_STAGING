## §0. What was done and found, in plain words, before any figure

The K-framework x86-64 semantics states one file per instruction
variant — one for `addl` with two 32-bit registers, another for the
same mnemonic with a memory operand, and so on, three thousand and
ninety-eight of them. Each file is a rewrite rule, and inside the rule
sits one closed expression per place the instruction writes: the
destination register, and each of the six flags. Those expressions are
built out of about thirty functions over fixed-width bit values, and the
files say at the top that they were generated by stratification — they
are Strata's learned formulas, tested against the chip, in K's form.

The first thing built was a grammar over that expression language: a
lexer, a recursive-descent parser and an evaluator into z3, written once
over the thirty functions and never per file. It reads a rule head to
learn the operand list, reads the one register-state update to learn
what the rule writes, and turns each written place into a z3 term. A
function it does not know it refuses by name and counts. Over every
regstate cell of all 3,098 files it knows thirty-six of the hundred and
twenty-six function names that appear; the ninety it does not are the
floating-point and division helpers, which their semantics leaves as
UNINTERPRETED symbols, and the vector shuffles.

The second thing was the join between the two readings' names. Their
file name and their rule head disagree about operand order — the name is
Intel's, destination first, and the head is the assembler's, source
first — so the shape is taken from the head, which is also the order our
own reference reads. Of their 3,070 instruction variants, 397 land on a
row our model table holds; the 2,673 that do not are listed by cause,
and the largest cause by far is that their semantics covers hundreds of
mnemonics our reference's table does not register at all.

The third thing was the comparison. Both readings are run over ONE set
of symbols — our reference seeds a free 64-bit symbol per register
family, and their rule's `getParentValue(R1, RSMap)` is bound to that
same symbol — so nothing has to be renamed afterwards and no
correspondence has to be assumed. Then z3 is asked, per written place,
whether the two terms can differ. Where their rule writes Intel's
undefined value the place is not compared, and where it is undefined
only on part of the input space the comparison is asked on the rest.

Four things came out of it worth saying before the numbers. First, the
check is possible at all and it is fast: 1,779 places decided in under
five seconds at a three-second solver ceiling, with not one `unknown`,
so the wider re-pose the brief allows never had to fire. Second, 760
places agree and 383 disagree, and the disagreements are not scattered —
they fall into a small number of mechanisms, each of which is a single
line of our reference. Third, the largest of those mechanisms is the
rule for writing a sub-64-bit result into a general register: our
reference zero-extends at every width, their reading keeps the upper
bits at widths 8 and 16, and that alone is 116 of the 383. Fourth, the
undefined regions come out as a table: twelve mnemonics leave something
undefined, and for the three shift mnemonics the undefined region is
PARTIAL and its condition is computed rather than assumed.

Two things did not go as the brief expected, and both are flagged rather
than worked around. The brief says "our 8,703 cells"; the model table on
disk today does not hold that number, because task m1b re-keyed it and
task ap2 rewrote it, and it now carries 5,912 distinct (mnem, shape,
key_width) triples with a translated row. And the brief asks for the
`imm_symbolic_gpr` spelling task ap5 added to the sweep; the table on
disk carries no row at that shape, so every immediate cell fell back to
the shape that bakes one literal in, and what those cells prove is
narrower than it would otherwise be.

## §2. The bit-index convention, stated before anything is compared

K's `MInt` indexes bits FROM THE MOST SIGNIFICANT END, and the range is
half open: `extractMInt(v, i, j)` of an n-bit `v` is the bits at
most-significant positions `i` through `j-1`, which in z3's
least-significant numbering is `Extract(n - 1 - i, n - j, v)`.

**LITERAL, and this is the one fragment of their text this log quotes**
(`/sources/X86-64-semantics/semantics/registerInstructions/addl_r32_r32.k`,
NCSA licence, quoted here because the brief asks for the convention
LITERAL with one worked example; line breaks added, nothing else
changed):

```
"CF" |-> extractMInt( addMInt( concatenateMInt( mi(1, 0),
         extractMInt( getParentValue(R1, RSMap), 32, 64)),
         concatenateMInt( mi(1, 0), extractMInt( getParentValue(R2,
         RSMap), 32, 64))), 0, 1)
```

**GLOSS, with values in motion.** Take `R1` to be our `%rsi` holding
`0x00000000ffffffff` and `R2` to be our `%rdi` holding
`0x0000000000000001`, and walk the expression inside out:

| step | what it is | value |
|---|---|---|
| `extractMInt(getParentValue(R1), 32, 64)` | positions 32..63 counted from the top of 64 bits, which are the LOW 32 | `0xffffffff` |
| `concatenateMInt(mi(1,0), that)` | a 0 bit prepended, so 33 bits | `0x0ffffffff` |
| the same two steps for `R2` | | `0x000000001` |
| `addMInt` of the two | a 33-bit sum | `0x100000000` |
| `extractMInt(sum, 0, 1)` | position 0 counted from the TOP of 33 bits, which is the carry out | `1` |
| `extractMInt(sum, 1, 33)` | positions 1..32 from the top, which are the low 32 | `0x00000000` |

So `CF` is 1 and the destination is 0 — the machine's own answer for
`0xffffffff + 1` at 32 bits.

**THE SAME CONVENTION CHECKED A SECOND WAY, from their own text.** Their
`notl_r32` rule writes `negMInt(extractMInt(getParentValue(R1), 32,
64))` and their `negl_r32` rule writes `addMInt(mi(32,1),
negMInt(...))`. So `negMInt` is the BITWISE complement and `negl` is the
two's complement built from it — which is only consistent under the
most-significant-first reading above. The grammar's `apply_function`
records exactly this, and cites their two rules as the definition.

**LITERAL**, same lane, step [2/9] — the whole rule run at those two
values by the grammar, every place it writes:

```

**GLOSS.** Every one of the six is the machine's answer for that
addition: the sum is zero, so `ZF` is 1 and `SF` is 0; the low byte is
`0x00`, which has even parity, so `PF` is 1; the low nibble carried, so
`AF` is 1; the two operands have unlike signs, so there is no signed
overflow and `OF` is 0.

## §3. The grammar, and what it does not know

The parser is written over the rule language, never per file. It slices
each file into the operand list inside the first `execinstr (...)` head
and the one `<regstate>` cell carrying an `updateMap`, which keeps K's
module headers and its `<k>` cell out of the expression grammar
entirely, and which is where a file stating two different register-state
updates is refused BY CAUSE rather than half read. Intel's undefined
value becomes a FRESH FREE SYMBOL, which is what "undefined" means, and
`undefined_region` then computes the condition under which a place
depends on one — so a wholly undefined flag and a partly undefined one
are told apart by measurement rather than by assumption.

### §7.2 `adc` and `sbb` leave a flag triple without the carry they read — 204 places over five flags

Our reference's `build_carry_binary` (`reference.py`, quoted in full):

> ```
> def build_carry_binary(ops):
>     """`adc`/`sbb` read the carry the previous flag-setting opcode
>     left, and set the flags from their own arithmetic."""
>     width = ops.destination_width()
>     left = ops.read(1, width)
>     right = ops.read(0, width)

### §7.3 The flag triple's two sides are read as a subtraction whatever the setter did — `add` and `neg`

Our reference holds the flags as `(setter, left, right)` and rebuilds a
bit on demand. `condition_table.cond_to_z3` computes every condition on
`left - right`:

> ```
> def cond_to_z3(cond, L, R, z3mod):
>     z3 = z3mod
>     diff = L - R
>     if cond == "CondEQ":
>         return L == R
> ```

For the `sub` and `cmp` family that IS the result, and those rows agree.
For `add` the result is `left + right`, and for `neg` it is `-left`,
while the triple carries `(add, left, right)` and `(neg, value, 0)`.
Three examples, **LITERAL**:

| variant | our line | place | ours | theirs | at the counterexample |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `addb_m8_r8` | `add %sil,(%rax)` | `flags:ZF` | `If(Extract(7, 0, seed_MEM__rax_) == Extract(7, 0, seed_rsi), 1, 0)` | `If(Extract(7, 0, seed_rsi) == 255*Extract(7, 0, seed_MEM__rax_), 1, 0)` | ours `0x1`, theirs `0x0` at `seed_MEM__rax_` = `0x8`, `seed_rsi` = `0x8` |
| `addb_m8_r8` | `add %sil,(%rax)` | `flags:SF` | `If(0 <= Extract(7, 0, seed_MEM__rax_) + 255*Extract(7, 0, seed_rsi), 0, 1)` | `Extract(7, 7, Concat(0, Extract(7, 0, seed_rsi)) + Concat(0, Extract(7, 0, seed_MEM__rax_)))` | ours `0x1`, theirs `0x0` at `seed_MEM__rax_` = `0x80`, `seed_rsi` = `0x88` |
| `negl_r32` | `neg %edi` | `flags:SF` | `If(0 <= Extract(31, 0, seed_rdi), 0, 1)` | `Extract(31, 31, 1 + ~Extract(31, 0, seed_rdi))` | ours `0x0`, theirs `0x1` at `seed_rdi` = `0x40000000` |

equal" and `SF` as "the first operand is negative"; theirs reads them off
the sum. For `neg`, ours reads `SF` off the ORIGINAL value and theirs off
the negated one.

## §11. Decided, recorded for audit

- **The shape of a variant is read from their rule HEAD, not from their
  file name**, because the two are in opposite operand orders (§4). The
  name remains the map's key.
- **A vector operand is bound to a 256-bit value whose low 128 bits are
  our own seed and whose upper 128 are a fresh unconstrained symbol**,
  because their `getParentValue(%xmm0, RSMap)` is the whole `YMM`
  register and ours is the `XMM` half. Where their written place is then
  256 bits and ours 128, the comparison is asked on the low 128 — the
  bits both readings hold — and the row carries the sentence saying so
  in its `narrowed` field.
- **Intel's undefined value becomes a fresh free symbol, and the
  undefined region is COMPUTED** as the condition under which forcing
  that symbol low and forcing it high give different terms (§8). A
  wholly undefined place is `undefined`; a partly undefined one is
  compared on the complement.
- **A flag-reading variant is run after one seeding comparison, `cmp
  %r10d,%r11d`**, whose two register families no operand shape of our
  sweep spells, and their `getFlag(F, RSMap)` is bound to our bit for
  `F` under that seed. The narrowing this causes is stated in §9.
- **A memory operand's arrival value is taken BEFORE the line runs.**
  `MachineState.memory_cell` puts the arrival symbol into `state.memory`
  on first touch and the write replaces it, so a binding made afterwards
  would bind their `Mem32` — the value the instruction READ — to the
  value it WROTE. This is the same correction task m1 made in its own
  program (log_236 §9, first item); it is made here in this task's
  program and not in a shared file.
- **z3's own words are the verdict vocabulary** — `unsat`, `sat`,

  outcome name was coined.
- **The side-by-side printing is z3's own one-line printing with the
  register symbols keeping our reference's names**, not the pipeline's

## §12. Awaiting the owner — flags for the coordinator

Five, each with its evidence above it, and none of them worked around.

1. **The sub-64-bit write rule** (§7.1). `reference.full64` zero-extends
   a write of any width below 64 into a general register; an independent
   reading keeps the bits above the written width at 8 and 16, and
   zero-extends only at 32. 116 places, 27 mnemonics, and the largest
   attested cell on it is `setne gpr_one 8` with 10,335 corpus ledger
   rows. Not decided here.
2. **`adc` and `sbb` leave a flag triple without the carry they read**
   (§7.2). `build_carry_binary` puts the incoming carry into the
   destination and not into `state.flags`, so every flag a later
   instruction reads after an `adc` or `sbb` is computed without it. 204
   places over `CF`, `OF`, `ZF`, `SF` and `PF`; `sbb`'s cells carry 2,510
   corpus ledger rows and `adc`'s 520.
3. **The flag triple's two sides are read as a subtraction whatever the
   setter did** (§7.3). `condition_table.cond_to_z3` computes every
   condition on `L - R`. That is the result for `sub` and `cmp` and
   those agree; for `add` the result is `L + R` and for `neg` it is
   `-L`, and `ZF`, `SF` and `PF` disagree there. Whether the corpus ever
   reads a flag after an `add` is a separate question this task did not
   measure; log_237 §3 lists the eight setters the corpus attests for
   `setne`, and `add` is not among them.
4. **`sub` and `sbb` are missing from `WIDTH_IS_NOT_A_SUFFIX`** (§7.4).
   `Operands.width_at` reads their last letter as an operand-size
   suffix, so `sub %esi,(%rax)` is modelled at 8 bits. Measured: 12
   disagreeing places on `sub` at `gpr_mem`. Thirty-four table
   mnemonics end in a size letter and are not in that list; for most of
   them the letter IS the suffix, and the full list is in the lane log
   named in §7.4.
5. **The model table on disk is not the one the brief describes.** The
   brief says 8,703 cells; the table carries 5,912 (mnem, shape,
   key_width) triples with a translated row, because task m1b re-keyed
   it and tasks ap5 and ap2 rewrote it. Related: the four
   `imm_symbolic_*` spellings task ap5 added to
   `model_translate.shapes_for` have no rows in the table, so it was
   never re-swept after that change, and every immediate cell in this
   check carries a verdict at one immediate rather than at every
   immediate (§9).
   Re-sweeping the table is a change to an artifact this brief does not
   name, so it was not done.
