## 1. What the objects are
- **Our reading**: `reference.py`'s builder per mnemonic, over VEX's
  semantics, giving one z3 term per written place at each (mnemonic,
  operand form, width) — 8,703 translated cells, the model table.
- **Their reading**: the K-framework x86-64 semantics (Dasgupta et al.,
  PLDI 2019), ~1,147 register-operand rules, 320 immediate, 1,474 memory,
  one file per instruction VARIANT (`addl_r32_r32.k`, `shrq_cl_r64.k`,
  `imulw_imm8_r16_r16.k` …), each a K rule whose `<regstate>` update is a
  closed expression over ~30 functions — `extractMInt(x, i, j)` (K's MInt
  indexes bits from the MOST significant: `extractMInt(r64, 32, 64)` is
  the LOW 32 bits), `concatenateMInt`, `addMInt subMInt mulMInt negMInt
  andMInt orMInt xorMInt shiftLeftMInt lshrMInt aShiftRightMInt uremMInt
  eqMInt ultMInt ugtMInt ugeMInt uleMInt sltMInt sgtMInt svalueMInt
  uvalueMInt bitwidthMInt`, `#ifMInt … #then … #else … #fi`, `mi(w, v)`
  literals, `andBool orBool notBool xorBool ==Bool`, `undefMInt` /
  `undefBool` (Intel's "undefined"), `getParentValue(R, RSMap)` (the full
  64-bit register an operand belongs to), `convToRegKeys(R)` (the register
  written), and flag keys `"CF" "PF" "AF" "ZF" "SF" "OF"`. The files say
  "Autogenerated using stratification": these ARE Strata's learned
  formulas, tested against the chip, in K form.
- **The check**: for every variant both readings hold, z3 asks whether our
  term and theirs are equal on every input, per written place — the
  destination and each flag they define. Where they write `undefMInt`,
  the flag is UNDEFINED: not compared, and recorded as the undefined
  region. This is the oracle the line was founded on, applied to level 0.
